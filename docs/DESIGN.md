## 1. Market analysis

### 1.1 Who is already in this market

| Player | Model | Price | What it is actually good at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Classplus** | White-label branded app, sales-led | **₹3–9 lakh/yr** for white-label tiers | Branded mobile app, onboarding hand-holding, CRM/marketing |
| **Teachmint** | Academic ERP | ~₹4K–15K/mo | Institute infrastructure — attendance, multi-teacher, live classes |
| **Graphy / Vedamo** | Creator-course platforms | ~₹4K–15K/mo | Individual educators selling courses |
| **TuitionPlus / Classpro** | Tuition-centre ops | Lower | Fees, attendance, parent communication — narrow and deep |
| **Moodle** | Open source | Free | Institutional standard, plugin ecosystem, SCORM |
| **Frappe LMS** | Open source (AGPL) | Free | Modern UI, complete data model, site-per-tenant |
| **Infix / Rocket LMS** | CodeCanyon scripts | ~$60 one-time + addons | Cheap self-hosted starting point |
| **Eklavvya** | Standalone AI exam generation | Per-use | NEP-aligned AI question papers, proctoring |

### 1.2 What the market data actually says

Four findings that change the design:

**a) There is an enormous price umbrella.** Classplus charges ₹3–9 lakh/year for
its white-label tier, and importantly **does not bring students** — the
institute supplies its own. That means the product is pure tooling, with no
network effect defending it. A solo operator can undercut that by an order of
magnitude and lose nothing, because there is no discovery advantage to compete
against.

**b) WhatsApp is not a feature, it is the channel.** Reported engagement is
**90%+ on WhatsApp versus 25–30% on email**, and live-class attendance moves
from **47% to 71%** when reminders run through WhatsApp automation. Every
serious Indian player reaches students through WATI, Interakt, or AiSensy.
Notably, Infix sells **WhatsappSupport as a paid addon** — people pay extra for
it. Any Indian LMS that treats email as the primary channel is designed for the
wrong country.

**c) Fee installments are core, not billing polish.** Indian coaching fees are
routinely collected in instalments, and Infix sells **Installment**,
**Invoice**, and **Tax** as three separate paid addons. TuitionPlus and Classpro
exist as entire companies built mostly on fee collection. A payments module
that only does one-time purchase does not fit how these institutes operate.

**d) The teacher pain is quantified.** A teacher reported spending **three full
weekends every semester** setting question papers for three classes. AI paper
generation from the teacher's own notes cuts that to minutes, and it is
strongest exactly where Indian coaching lives — MCQs, numericals, prelims-style
objective items — and weakest on written-argument answers.

### 1.3 What the owned addon catalogue proves

The Infix Universal Plugins Bundle is a list of things customers **paid extra
for**. That is stronger evidence than any feature survey:

| Addon | What it signals |
|---|---|
| Installment, Invoice, Tax | Indian fee reality: instalments + GST |
| WhatsappSupport | The channel that actually works |
| BBB, Jitsi, GoogleMeet, InAppLiveClass | **Four** live-class options — institutes want provider choice, not lock-in |
| AIContent | AI is already being sold into this market |
| Subscription | Recurring revenue models |
| Affiliate, Cashback, Gift, RegistrationBonus | Referral growth is how institutes recruit |
| Store | Institutes sell physical study material |
| Chat, Forum, SupportTicket, Noticeboard | Communication surfaces |
| Appointment | 1-on-1 doubt sessions are a paid product |
| Organization, UserType | B2B / multi-branch structures |
| SCORM | Enterprise and compliance buyers |
| BunnyStorage, AmazonS3 | Bunny is already validated in this exact market |
| TwoFA, GoogleCalendar | Hygiene |

### 1.4 The gap — the differentiation thesis

Every incumbent is strong on **one** of these three and weak on the others:

1. **India-native operations** — instalments, GST, WhatsApp, attendance, parents
2. **AI that is actually load-bearing** — not a chatbot bolted onto the corner
3. **A price a 200-student institute can pay**

- Classplus/Teachmint: strong on 1, weak on 2, fail 3 badly.
- Moodle/Frappe: free (3), but zero India-native operations (1) and no AI (2).
- Eklavvya-type tools: strong on 2, but standalone — disconnected from the
  content, the batch, and the student record.
- Infix/Rocket: cheap (3), decent on 1 via addons, but AI is a bolt-on and the
  codebase is a generic marketplace script.

**Nobody occupies all three at once.** That intersection is the thesis of this
product:

> An LMS where the AI is the ingestion layer every piece of content passes
> through, wrapped in Indian coaching-institute operations (instalments, GST,
> WhatsApp, attendance), priced where a 200-student institute can say yes
> without a purchase committee.

**The specific unfair advantage** is that once content is ingested once, question
paper generation, doubt-solving, revision notes, search, and lecture summaries
all become nearly free — because they are read paths over one artifact rather
than five separate AI products. Eklavvya has to re-ingest for every paper.
Classplus has no ingestion layer at all.

## 2. Architecture — the AI ingestion spine

The prior spec put AI in Phase 5 as four independent features. That is inverted
here. **Every piece of content entering the system passes through one pipeline
at upload time**, producing a durable derived artifact.

```
upload  (video · PDF · notes · recorded class · question bank)
   │
   ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  INGESTION PIPELINE   (queued · idempotent · resumable)     │
│                                                             │
│  transcript ─► clean+chunk ─► embeddings ─► key concepts    │
│                    │                                        │
│                    ├─► syllabus mapping (chapter/topic)     │
│                    ├─► difficulty tagging                   │
│                    └─► seed question bank (MCQ/num/short)   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
   │
   ▼
lesson_artifacts   ◄── the durable spine, versioned per lesson
   │
   ├─► question paper generator   (blueprint-driven, teacher-editable)
   ├─► doubt-solving chat          (RAG, Hinglish + English)
   ├─► revision notes / summaries
   ├─► semantic search across the course
   ├─► weak-topic detection        (quiz results × concept map)
   └─► WhatsApp nudges             (grounded in what the student missed)
```

**Why this is the right shape:**

- **Cost.** One expensive pass per lesson, amortised across every student and
  every feature forever. The alternative re-derives the same understanding on
  every single request.
- **Consistency.** Quiz, chatbot, summary, and revision notes are grounded in
  the same artifact, so they cannot contradict one another.
- **No retrofit.** A sixth AI feature is a new read path, not a new pipeline.
- **Degrades safely.** If the AI provider is down, ingestion queues and retries;
  everything already ingested keeps serving every feature.
- **It is the moat.** Competitors bolt AI on per-feature. Ingestion-first means
  each new AI feature costs days instead of months.

**Non-negotiable engineering rules for the pipeline:**

1. Idempotent and resumable — re-running on the same lesson must not duplicate
   artifacts or double-bill.
2. Versioned — re-ingesting after a lesson edit creates a new artifact version;
   old quizzes keep pointing at the version they were generated from.
3. Per-tenant spend metering enforced **before** the call, not after. Without
   this a single abusive tenant is an unbounded liability.
4. Every AI-generated artifact is **teacher-editable and teacher-approved**
   before students see it. The product's promise is "saves you three weekends",
   not "replaces your judgement".

## 3. Tech stack

Chosen against three constraints: the owner must be able to **finish** it, it
must sell to **multiple institutes**, and the developer's background is
**PHP/WordPress** rather than Python or heavy JS.

| Layer | Choice | Why this and not the alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Backend | **Laravel** (current stable), PHP 8.3 | Closest to the owner's PHP background. Queues, auth, permissions, scheduling all first-class. Two of the studied products (Infix, Rocket) are Laravel, so their patterns are readable. |
| **Admin / staff UI** | **FilamentPHP** — multi-panel | **The single most important choice in this document.** See §3.1. |
| Learner UI | **Livewire 3 + Alpine + Tailwind** | Changed from Inertia 2 + React + TypeScript + shadcn/ui on 2026-08-08. Filament is itself Livewire, so all four UI surfaces share one paradigm, one build pipeline and one session model. The earlier choice put the least familiar technology (React + TypeScript) on the most critical screens — player, quiz runner, doubt chat. Accepted cost: an Alpine wrapper around `hls.js`, and a small fetch island for streaming doubt-chat replies. |
| Database | **PostgreSQL 16** | Changed from MySQL 8 by the eng review (decision E1). Native full-text removes a separate search service, and it is the same single database — not an extra one. |
| Vector search | **pgvector** (same Postgres instance) | Changed from MySQL 8 vector columns + `sqlite-vec` sidecar. The original justification ("avoid a dedicated vector DB") did not hold: Postgres **replaces** MySQL, so the service count is identical. pgvector reaches 95%+ recall at 1M vectors on default settings and is the documented production RAG path as of 2026; no comparable MySQL 8 track record exists. The `sqlite-vec` sidecar is dropped — it meant a second store, second backup, and artifacts split across two sources of truth. |
| Tenancy | **Single DB + `tenant_id` + global scope** | See §3.2. |
| Auth | Sanctum + `spatie/laravel-permission`, TOTP 2FA for admin roles | Standard, well-trodden. |
| Queue/cache | **Redis 7 + Laravel Horizon** | The ingestion pipeline is queue-shaped. Non-negotiable. |
| Video | **Bunny Stream**, behind a swappable `VideoProvider` interface | Verified against 2026 pricing: $0.005/GB storage + **$0.03/GB delivery (Asia)**. At 200 students × 10 hrs/mo (~1.4 TB) that is **≈$42/mo vs Vimeo Scale at $165/mo** — and Vimeo caps DRM behind Enterprise while auto-escalating you to a $6,000+/yr contract on overage. Cost is controlled by **GB delivered**, not by vendor: 480p default (≈3× saving), adaptive bitrate, per-plan resolution caps, PWA offline, and **per-tenant bandwidth metering that makes bandwidth an institute-billable resource**. See `FEATURES.md` §3.1. |
| Files / CDN | Bunny Storage + Bunny CDN | Same vendor, one API key. |
| Payments | **Razorpay** — one-time + **instalment plans** + GST invoicing | Instalments are core (§1.2c), not a v2 addon. |
| **WhatsApp** | **AiSensy or Interakt** (BSP) behind a provider interface | Core channel (§1.2b), in v1, not deferred. |
| Live classes | **`ExternalLinkProvider` day one**, behind a `LiveClassProvider` interface (+ Zoom Video SDK, Jitsi, BigBlueButton later) | Changed 2026-08-08. Self-hosting a WebRTC media server is an operations specialty, not a solo developer's side task, and BigBlueButton fails at 6pm while a class is running. Day one the teacher pastes their own Zoom or Meet link: zero setup, zero accounts. Zoom Video SDK is the paid step up and needs **no host licences** (it is a different product from Zoom accounts, which do charge per host). BigBlueButton gets self-hosted once an institute's volume justifies the operational load. **Consequence for S5:** attendance ships with two writers (manual marking, async video coverage), not three, until a webhook-capable provider is in place. |
| AI | **Claude API** — Haiku 4.5 / Sonnet 5 / Opus 5, routed per task | See §3.3. |
| Email | Resend or Brevo | Secondary channel. WhatsApp is primary. |
| Monitoring | Telescope (dev) + Sentry (from launch) | |
| Deploy | Ubuntu VPS via **Ploi** or **Laravel Forge** | Hand-provisioning Nginx/PHP-FPM/certs is a day that will not be spent. |

### 3.1 Why Filament is the decisive choice

The honest problem with building from scratch is the sheer volume of CRUD.
Frappe LMS ships **69 doctypes**; a competitive LMS needs a comparable data
model. Hand-building admin screens for ~60 entities is precisely the mountain
that killed the previous attempt at task 21 of 170.

Frappe's actual superpower is not its LMS features — it is the **doctype
system**: declare a model, get admin UI, list views, filters, permissions, and a
REST API for free.

**Filament is Laravel's equivalent of that.** One `Resource` class yields a
full CRUD panel — forms, tables, filters, bulk actions, relation managers —
wired into `spatie/laravel-permission`. Filament's multi-panel support maps
directly onto the four-tier role structure:

| Panel | Audience | Built with |
|---|---|---|
| `/super` | Platform owner — tenants, plans, billing, impersonation | Filament |
| `/admin` | Institute admin — branding, staff, batches, fees, reports | Filament |
| `/teach` | Instructor — courses, lessons, question bank, grading | Filament |
| `/` (learner) | Student — player, quiz, doubt chat, progress | Livewire + Alpine |
| `mobile/` | Student and parent on Android, later iOS | Capacitor shell over the same Livewire app |

This is what makes "build from scratch" a realistic plan rather than a repeat of
the last four attempts. It converts the 60-entity CRUD mountain from months of
hand-written screens into declarative resource classes, and reserves custom
frontend effort for the ~15% of screens where UX actually differentiates.

### 3.2 Why single-database tenancy

Frappe uses site-per-tenant (separate database each). The prior spec specified
database-per-tenant too. Before tenant #1 exists, that buys physical isolation
at the price of N migrations per deploy, N backup streams, N-way connection
management, and painful Windows local development.

Row-level isolation — a `tenant_id` column with a global Eloquent scope — gives
the identical product (subdomains, isolated data, per-institute branding) at a
fraction of the operational cost, **provided** the isolation is proven rather
than assumed:

> **Release gate:** an automated cross-tenant isolation test suite must pass in
> CI on every commit. It attempts to read and write across tenant boundaries
> through every model, every API route, and every Filament panel, and fails the
> build on any leak. This is not optional and does not get skipped to ship.

If an enterprise customer later contractually requires physical isolation, that
migration is bounded, known work funded by an actual contract.

### 3.3 AI provider, model routing, and cost

**Provider changed from the prior spec.** `skills.md` specified OpenAI GPT-4o —
a dated choice predating current model generations. This design uses the Claude
API. If the owner prefers OpenAI, the pipeline architecture is unchanged; only
the client library, model IDs, and the cost table below would need redoing.

| Task | Model | Rate (per MTok in/out) |
|---|---|---|
| Bulk chunking, tagging, routine doubts | `claude-haiku-4-5` | $1 / $5 |
| Question generation, summaries, key concepts | `claude-sonnet-5` | $3 / $15 |
| Full paper generation with difficulty balancing; hard doubt escalation | `claude-opus-5` | $5 / $25 |

Three API techniques are **requirements, not optimisations**:

1. **Hybrid routing — real-time and batch, split by deadline.** *(Corrected by CEO
   decision D7; this section previously said all ingestion routes through the Batch
   API, which contradicted the promise of a quiz appearing right after a class.)*
   Anything a user is waiting on runs real-time: post-class artifacts, doubt chat,
   a teacher generating a paper now. Bulk backfill of an existing library runs
   batched. The two paths are costed separately.
2. **Prompt caching** — cached reads cost ~0.1× base input. Per-course context
   is cached; this dominates doubt-chat economics.
3. **Adaptive thinking + `effort`** — `low`/`medium` for routine generation,
   `high` only where correctness genuinely matters (paper generation).

**Cost model** (≈₹88/USD; a 60-minute lecture ≈ 12k input tokens of transcript):

*Ingestion, one-time per lesson* — Sonnet 5, ~15k in / ~4k out, via Batch API:

```
(15,000 × $3/1M  +  4,000 × $15/1M) × 0.5  ≈  $0.05  ≈  ₹4.5 per lesson
```

A 100-lesson course: **≈ ₹450, once.** Negligible.

*Doubt chat, recurring* — per question, ~7k cached context + ~1k fresh + ~500 out:

| Model | Per question | 50 questions/student/month |
|---|---|---|
| Haiku 4.5 | ≈ $0.0042 (₹0.37) | **≈ ₹18** |
| Sonnet 5 | ≈ $0.0126 (₹1.10) | **≈ ₹55** |

Routing routine doubts to Haiku and escalating only hard ones gives a blended
**≈ ₹25–30 per student per month**.

Against a coaching fee of ₹500–₹2,000/student/month, AI cost is **1.5–6% of
revenue**. The unit economics work. Heavy-usage institutes are metered rather
than sold unlimited.

> Sonnet 5 carries introductory pricing of $2/$10 per MTok through
> **2026-08-31**. All figures above use standard $3/$15 so the model does not
> depend on promotional pricing expiring in three weeks.

## 5. Milestone definition — "Institute Zero"

The prior attempt's 170-task plan is replaced by one binary gate:

> **One institute, one real batch, real students, real fees collected.**

Done means all of:

- Institute onboarded with its own subdomain and branding
- ≥ 1 course with ≥ 10 lessons fully ingested
- ≥ 1 batch with ≥ 10 real students enrolled
- Fees collected through Razorpay, including at least one **instalment** plan
- Students have watched video and attempted an **AI-generated** quiz
- ≥ 50 real doubt-chat questions answered
- WhatsApp reminders firing on a real class schedule
- **The teacher states the AI question bank saved them measurable time** — the
  one qualitative signal that the thesis in §1.4 is real

**Blocking engineering gates:** cross-tenant isolation suite green in CI;
ingestion recovers from provider outage without data loss; per-tenant AI spend
capped and visible; video unavailable without signed URL + enrolment check.

## 7. Distribution and operations

Multi-tenant SaaS. No package or binary distribution.

- **Hosting:** single Ubuntu VPS via Ploi/Forge; scale vertically first
- **Domains:** `{institute}.yourdomain.com` wildcard DNS + wildcard TLS; custom
  domain mapping via CNAME + on-demand certificates
- **CI/CD:** GitHub Actions — tests (isolation suite included) on push,
  auto-deploy `main` to staging, manual promotion to production
- **Backups:** nightly encrypted PostgreSQL dumps to Bunny Storage, 30-day retention,
  with a **documented and rehearsed** restore (an untested backup is not a backup)
- **Rollback:** Forge/Ploi atomic deploys, one-command rollback

**Fixed monthly cost at Institute Zero scale:** VPS ~$10–20 · Ploi/Forge ~$10–15
· Bunny ~$5–10 · Sentry free tier · WhatsApp BSP ~₹1,000–2,500 · AI usage-based
→ **roughly $45–70/month plus AI.**

## 8. Blocking prerequisites and the assignment

**Before any code:**

1. **`git init` + first commit.** There is currently no version control — no
   review, no history, no rollback. This is the single cheapest risk reduction
   available and takes two minutes.
2. Local environment: Docker Desktop or WSL2 for PostgreSQL 16 (with the pgvector
   extension) + Redis 7 on Windows.
3. Start the **WhatsApp BSP application** (AiSensy/Interakt) — external approval
   with a lead time, so it must start before it is needed.

**The assignment — the thing this design cannot substitute for:**

Get **one institute to say yes to a free pilot** before, or in parallel with,
building. Not a sale, not a contract — one named institute that agrees to run
one batch on this, and one named person there who will answer the phone.

This week:

1. Write down the institute where the LMS problems were observed first-hand,
   plus two others within reach.
2. Name one actual person at each — teacher, academic head, or owner.
3. Ask one question, not a pitch: *"Aap abhi classes, notes aur fees kaise
   manage karte ho, aur usme sabse zyada time kahan jaata hai?"*
4. Write the answers down verbatim.
5. If question papers or repeated doubts come up as painful, ask: *"Agar main
   aisa system bana doon jo aapke apne notes se question paper bana de, kya aap
   ek batch pe try karoge?"*

**Why this and not "start coding":** the architecture above is deliberately
engineered to be finishable — Filament removes the CRUD mountain, the P1 list is
bounded, the ingestion spine makes AI features cheap. But finishable is not
finished. Four previous attempts had working code and nobody waiting. One person
expecting a demo on a date is the missing input, and no architecture substitutes
for it. If nobody says yes, that is the most valuable possible result — learned
in a week of conversations rather than six months of building.

2. **Pricing model** — metered AI usage argues for per-student per-month.
   Not needed to start building; needed before the first sales conversation.
   Anchor: Classplus at ₹3–9 lakh/yr leaves enormous room underneath.
3. **Language depth** — is Hinglish doubt-solving enough, or is full Devanagari
   UI localisation needed? Materially affects content and QA scope.
4. **Which institute is Institute Zero?** Unanswered, and the highest-risk open
   item in this document. See §8.

## 11. Codebase organisation — split by domain, not by layer

Decided 2026-08-09. The question raised was whether to separate `frontend/` and
`backend/` into two folders. With Livewire that split does not exist: a Livewire
component is a PHP class and a Blade view acting as one unit, and Filament is the
same. Separating them would mean returning to a React SPA plus REST API, which
would bring back the ~60-entity hand-built admin CRUD that Filament exists to
remove.

The separation that does pay here is **by feature domain**.

```
lms/
├── app/
│   ├── Domains/          eight self-contained feature folders
│   ├── Integrations/     vendor interfaces + drivers (shared)
│   ├── Support/          circuit breaker, cache, tenancy (shared)
│   └── Http/             middleware, base controllers (shared)
├── mobile/               Capacitor — the one real frontend split
├── routes/               split per surface
├── tests/                + Isolation/ and Evals/
└── docs/
```

### The eight domains map one-to-one onto the eight build slices

| Domain | Owns | Slice |
|---|---|---|
| `Tenancy` | Institutes, users, roles, permissions, branding, tenant scope | S1 |
| `Content` | Courses, sections, lessons, video, documents, watch tracking | S2 |
| `Ingestion` | The AI spine — transcripts, chunks, embeddings, artifacts, doubt chat | S3 |
| `Enrolment` | Batches, seats, waitlist, timetable, attendance | S4 |
| `LiveClass` | Live sessions, join flow, recordings, webhook attendance | S5 |
| `Assessment` | Question bank, quizzes, AI papers, grading, certificates | S6 |
| `Billing` | Fees, instalments, invoices, defaulters, refunds | S7 |
| `Comms` | WhatsApp, notifications, parent portal, reports, dashboards | S8 |

Build order and folder layout become the same map. Finishing a slice means one
folder is complete, which is also what the fortnightly demo shows.

### Every domain has the same eight subfolders

`Models/` · `Services/` · `Filament/` · `Livewire/` · `Views/` · `Jobs/` ·
`Policies/` · `Events/`

Learn the shape once and you know where anything is in all eight. Everything about
fees — the model, the schedule logic, the admin screen, the student screen, the
invoice PDF, the reminder job — sits in `app/Domains/Billing/`.

### Two rules that keep the separation real

**Domains communicate through events, not by calling each other's services.**
`Billing` fires `InstalmentOverdue`; `Comms` listens and sends the WhatsApp
message. `Billing` does not know `Comms` exists. Without this rule the eight
folders are separate in name only.

**Every integration ships a `FakeDriver`.** This is what allows the system to be
built and tested with no Bunny account, no Razorpay key, and no WhatsApp BSP
approval. BSP approval takes weeks; the fake driver means that wait blocks nothing.

### One accepted deviation from stock Laravel

Blade views live inside their domain (`app/Domains/Billing/Views/`) rather than in
`resources/views/`. That needs one `View::addNamespace()` call per domain,
registered in a loop at startup. Taken deliberately: the alternative splits every
feature across two distant folders permanently. Because it is non-standard, it
belongs in `CLAUDE.md` where anyone reading the code will find it.

Full folder-by-folder tables, including routes, tests and the mobile layout, are
in the product map artifact.
